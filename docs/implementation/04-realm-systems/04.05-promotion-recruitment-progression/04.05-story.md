# Story 04.05: Promotion recruitment progression

As a player, I want stronger promotion choices to require sustained realm control so that immediately rushing a Pawn to promotion is not automatically the dominant strategy.

## Status

- [ ] In progress

## Implementation notes

- Task 04.05.01 added the versioned 2/4/8 scenario contract and a shared deterministic realm-control score; promotion enforcement remains pending.

## Proposed rules contract

- Promotion eligibility uses the promoting player's current realm-control score at the owner-turn boundary: each owned settlement contributes 1 point, current governance of that owned settlement contributes 1 additional point, and establishment of that owned settlement contributes 2 additional points.
- Knights are always available. Bishops unlock at score 2, Rooks at score 4, and Queens at score 8. Unlocks are cumulative.
- Control is current rather than historical. Losing or transferring settlements, governance, or establishment can relock kinds for future promotion batches without changing choices that are already queued.
- Owner-turn processing resolves transfers and completes settlement cycles before calculating the score, so an establishment or transfer completed at that boundary counts immediately.
- The score and allowed kinds are calculated once for every promotion becoming ready at the same owner-turn boundary. Each queued choice stores that shared snapshot, preventing an earlier promotion from unlocking a stronger choice later in the batch.
- Thresholds are scenario rules. Missing configuration uses the initial 2/4/8 ladder, and Introductory, Standard, and Large scenarios author those values explicitly.
- The initial score deliberately excludes elapsed turns, piece material, attacked squares, relative advantage, production readiness, and supported Pawns. These remain possible later balance extensions rather than implicit inputs.

## Map-control options considered

- **Weighted settlement control (selected):** combines ownership, live governance, and establishment using concepts already present in canonical state. It rewards both expansion and the ability to defend developed territory.
- **Fixed settlement milestones:** unlock by the number of claimed or established settlements. This is simpler, but cannot distinguish a fragile claim from a governed, established realm.
- **Board influence:** count occupied, attacked, or protected squares. This is tactically responsive, but volatile and risks making powerful promoted pieces generate the control needed to recruit more powerful pieces.
- **Strategic regions or terrain:** unlock by controlling authored areas or terrain types. This could create distinctive maps, but requires new region metadata and map-specific explanations.
- **Connected realm or supply lines:** reward settlements joined by controlled routes. This encourages coherent territory, but needs new connectivity and interruption rules.
- **Relative control:** compare a player's score with the opponent's. This could support comeback tuning, but makes a player's available promotion choices change because of distant enemy state and is harder to forecast.

The selected absolute score is deliberately small and explainable. The scenario-authored threshold contract leaves room for later variants without silently changing the initial metric.

## Acceptance criteria

- At scores 0-1 only Knight is available; Bishop joins at 2, Rook at 4, and Queen at 8.
- Promotion eligibility follows current owned, governed, and established settlement state and can relock for later batches.
- Transfers and establishment completed at an owner-turn boundary affect promotions queued at that boundary.
- Every promotion queued in one boundary has the same frozen score and allowed kinds, regardless of how earlier choices in the batch change board control.
- Local, online, saved, restored, reconnected, and replayed matches enforce identical promotion choices.
- All shipped scenarios use the progression rule and can reach every tier.

## Cross-cutting concerns

- Score calculation, allowed-kind ordering, choice ordering, serialization, and hashing remain deterministic.
- The authoritative reducer is the only source of promotion legality; client filtering provides feedback but not authority.
- A player can always inspect their own frozen promotion eligibility. Future fog-of-war projections must not expose unrelated enemy realm state while doing so.
- Locked choices use text or symbols as well as color and fit within the client-owned interaction region.
- AI action enumeration must consume the frozen legal choices instead of assuming all four promotion kinds.
