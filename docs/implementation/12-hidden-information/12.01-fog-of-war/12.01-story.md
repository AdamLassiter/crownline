# Story 12.01: Fog of war

As a player, I want to explore an initially unknown battlefield and see enemy pieces only while my army can currently observe them.

This story is an opt-in post-release rules variant. It deliberately supersedes the GDD and player-guide statements that the initial release has no hidden information; those documents remain correct until this story is completed and a fog-enabled scenario is selected.

## Status

- [ ] In progress

## Proposed rules contract

- Every square starts `Undiscovered` for each player. Before the first seat view is presented, the starting army's current vision is applied, so initially visible squares immediately become explored.
- A fog-enabled scenario declares one positive whole-square `vision_radius`. The first authored tuning baseline is radius 3; it remains scenario data rather than a hard-coded rules constant.
- Vision uses Chebyshev distance from every living friendly piece, producing a square radius that is inexpensive and intuitive on the chess grid. Terrain, pieces, walls, rivers, and other edges do not block vision in this first version; line-of-sight vision is explicitly out of scope.
- A square in current vision is `Visible`. Once a square has ever been visible it remains `Explored`, permanently revealing its terrain and static site information. It returns to `Explored` when no friendly piece currently sees it.
- Enemy pieces and dynamic enemy realm information are visible only on currently visible squares. They leave no last-known-position ghost when vision is lost. Friendly pieces remain visible because every friendly piece sees its own square.
- Check, active player, clocks, draw state, and terminal outcome remain public match facts. Enemy settlement ownership/progress, governors, promotion candidates, and other dynamic details are redacted unless currently visible; static discovered settlement/site identity remains known.
- Full canonical rules still decide movement, capture, check, governance, and outcomes. The client must not receive canonical legal-move previews that disclose hidden blockers. A rejected intent uses one opaque public error, although the design explicitly accepts that a player may infer limited information from action outcomes rather than changing chess movement or charging a turn for a rejected move.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each player has an independent, deterministic three-state visibility map.
- Terrain and static sites remain known after exploration; enemy pieces and dynamic enemy state do not.
- No local handoff frame, online payload, UI panel, overlay, transition, log, or active-match export reveals information outside the viewing seat's projection.
- Saves, journals, reconnects, and replays reconstruct identical per-seat exploration knowledge.
- Perfect-information scenarios behave byte-for-byte as before unless a versioned migration explicitly requires otherwise.

## Cross-cutting concerns

- The canonical state remains complete and deterministic while every presentation boundary becomes seat-aware.
- Visibility must not rely on color alone: undiscovered, explored, and visible states need distinct luminance plus texture/symbol treatment.
- Per-seat masks and projections are revision-cached and bounded by the maximum 24x24 board.
- Active-match diagnostics and exports are treated as disclosure surfaces, not merely developer tooling.
